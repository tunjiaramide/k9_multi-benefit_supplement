Loose stools, an unsettled stomach or changes in digestion after switching dog food can leave any pet owner wondering what to do. Probiotics are often mentioned as a possible source of digestive support, but what do they actually do, and what should you look for when buying a supplement?

## What Are Probiotics?

Probiotics are live microorganisms that, when provided in adequate amounts, can offer health benefits. In dog supplements, they're used to support a healthy balance of bacteria in the digestive system.

However, not all probiotic products are the same. The strains used, the amount provided and the quality of the formulation all matter.

## Understanding CFU Counts

You'll often see probiotics measured in CFU, or colony-forming units. This indicates the number of viable microorganisms in a serving.

A higher CFU count doesn't automatically mean a product is better. Look for clear information about the amount provided, and check whether the label identifies the probiotic strains. These details help you compare products more meaningfully.

## When Might Dogs Need Digestive Support?

Probiotics may be considered in several situations:

- **Food changes:** Switching foods can temporarily affect digestion, especially if the transition happens too quickly.
- **Digestive inconsistency:** Recurring changes in stool may prompt owners to explore digestive support, although persistent problems need veterinary assessment.
- **After antibiotics:** Antibiotics can affect gut bacteria, but it's best to ask your veterinarian whether a probiotic is appropriate.
- **Everyday wellness:** Some owners include probiotics in their dog's regular nutritional routine.

Probiotics aren't a cure for every digestive problem. Persistent diarrhoea, repeated vomiting, blood in the stool or a loss of appetite should be assessed by a veterinarian.

## Probiotics vs. Prebiotics

The names sound similar, but they serve different purposes.

**Probiotics** are live microorganisms. **Prebiotics** are substances, often certain types of fibre, that beneficial gut microorganisms can use.

Some products contain both. Checking the label helps you understand what a supplement actually provides.

## What to Check Before Buying in Nigeria

Before choosing a probiotic supplement, consider these points:

- **CFU per serving:** Check the amount stated on the label.
- **Strain information:** Look for specific strains where available, rather than relying only on the words "probiotic blend."
- **Storage requirements:** Heat and humidity can affect some probiotic products, so follow storage instructions and buy from sellers who handle products appropriately.
- **Ease of use:** A product that fits easily into your dog's feeding routine may be simpler to give consistently.

If you're changing your dog's food, introduce the new food gradually over several days or longer, depending on your dog's needs. A probiotic doesn't replace a careful transition.
