As dogs get older, or simply become less active than they used to be, their mobility can become a concern. This is one reason joint supplements are popular among dog owners.

But with so many products available, how do you know what to look for? Start with the ingredients, their amounts and what the supplement is actually intended to do.

## Three Common Joint-Support Ingredients

### Glucosamine

Glucosamine is a naturally occurring compound found in the body and is commonly included in supplements formulated to support joint health. Glucosamine HCl is one form you'll often see on dog supplement labels. Our [glucosamine guide](/blog/glucosamine-for-dogs-nigeria) covers it in more detail.

### Chondroitin

Chondroitin sulfate is frequently paired with glucosamine in joint-support formulas. Both ingredients are commonly used in products intended to support joint function and mobility.

### MSM

MSM, short for methylsulfonylmethane, is a sulfur-containing compound often included alongside glucosamine and chondroitin.

These ingredients are popular in joint supplements, but their presence alone doesn't guarantee a product will improve your dog's mobility. Evidence of benefit varies, so it's worth looking beyond the ingredient list.

## Which Dogs Might Need Joint Support?

Joint health is particularly relevant for:

- **Large and giant breeds**, such as German Shepherds, Rottweilers and Labradors.
- **Senior dogs**, whose mobility may change with age.
- **Active and working dogs**, whose daily activities place repeated demands on their bodies.

However, breed and age alone don't mean every dog needs a joint supplement. Your veterinarian can help determine whether supplementation makes sense for your dog's individual circumstances.

## What Makes a Good Joint Supplement?

Before choosing a product, consider these four things.

### 1. The actual ingredient amounts

Check how many milligrams of glucosamine, chondroitin and MSM are provided per serving. A product listing these ingredients without disclosing their amounts makes meaningful comparisons difficult.

### 2. The recommended serving

Check how much your dog needs each day and whether the product is suitable for their size and needs.

### 3. Realistic product claims

Be cautious of supplements promising to cure joint disease, reverse damage or restore mobility. Nutritional supplements are not substitutes for veterinary diagnosis or treatment.

### 4. Overall joint care

Maintaining a healthy weight, providing appropriate exercise and seeking veterinary advice when mobility changes can all play important roles in supporting your dog's joints. A supplement alone cannot address every cause of joint discomfort.

## Keeping Track of Your Dog's Mobility

If you're monitoring your dog's mobility, pay attention to everyday activities. Are they getting up comfortably after resting? Are they still happy to climb stairs or go for walks?

Noticing changes over time can help you explain your concerns to your veterinarian. Just remember that apparent improvements or setbacks aren't enough on their own to determine whether a supplement is working.
